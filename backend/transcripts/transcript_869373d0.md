### Legal Theory
May it please the Court, I strongly oppose this application for anticipatory bail. Section 438 CrPC is an extraordinary and discretionary remedy, to be granted only in exceptional cases, and the verified record before this Court discloses no such exceptional circumstance.

### Submissions
1. The material here is clear. The respondent union filed an application under **Section 11-C of the U.P. Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 read with Section 13-A of the Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act, 1946**, seeking a declaration that **15 persons appointed on contract basis as drivers and conductors** should be treated as regular and substantive workmen with consequential benefits. The Labour Court allowed that application and even directed minimum wages admissible to regular employees. But the Supreme Court has already held in this very matter that the scope of **Section 11-C** is much narrower than a regular reference under **Section 10 of the Industrial Disputes Act** or **Section 4-K of the U.P. Industrial Disputes Act**. It also held that the Labour Court could not grant the relief it did, because the order was based on **Rule 2 of the 1981 Regulations**, which clearly excludes contract employees, and the Labour Court had no power to amend those Regulations in a Section 11-C proceeding.
2. So the core point is this: the underlying order was beyond the limited statutory jurisdiction invoked. A party cannot seek to convert that narrow interpretative jurisdiction into a route for substantive regularization, and then ask this Court to extend the extraordinary protection of anticipatory bail as if the statutory limits did not exist.
3. I fairly state that the record does not disclose arrest, proclamation, recovery, or other facts warranting a custodial-interrogation submission, and I do not overstate that ground. But even without that, the applicant has shown no exceptional circumstance to justify pre-arrest protection.
4. In view of the gravity of the statutory overreach, the binding holdings already recorded in this very matter, and the absence of any exceptional circumstance, the prosecution humbly prays that this Anticipatory Bail application be rejected.
- **Section 438 CrPC** — anticipatory bail; extraordinary discretionary relief.
- **Section 11-C, U.P. Industrial Disputes Act, 1947** — reference confined to questions regarding application or interpretation of a standing order; decision final and binding.
- **Section 13-A, Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act, 1946** — similar limited reference on application or interpretation of a certified standing order.
- **Section 10, Industrial Disputes Act, 1947** — regular reference mechanism, broader than Section 11-C / 13-A.
- **Section 4-K, U.P. Industrial Disputes Act, 1947** — corresponding regular reference provision under the U.P. Act, broader than Section 11-C.
- **Rule 2, U.P.S.R.T.C. Employees (Other than Officers) Service Regulations, 1981** — expressly excludes employees engaged on contract basis.
- **Section 11-C / Section 13-A scope limit** — only application/interpretation of a standing order; not substantive regularization or amendment of service rules.
- No verified POCSO, Dowry Prohibition Act, IPC, or IEA presumptive provisions arise on this record.
The verified facts show that the union sought a declaration treating **15 contract drivers and conductors** as regular and substantive workmen with consequential benefits. It is undisputed that they were **appointed on contract basis**, and the Corporation had specifically relied on **Rule 2 of the 1981 Regulations**, which excludes contract employees. The Labour Court nevertheless granted relief on **19 September 2001**, but the Supreme Court has already held in this very matter that such relief was beyond the scope of **Section 11-C** and **Section 13-A**.
5. That matters for anticipatory bail because the applicant is asking this Court to extend pre-arrest protection despite a record showing a **jurisdictional transgression** by the very forum whose order was challenged. The Supreme Court’s own findings establish that the Labour Court was not dealing with a certified standing order at all; it relied on the Regulations and could not amend them in Section 11-C proceedings. Therefore, the applicant cannot invoke equity as though the underlying action was legally regular.
6. I must fairly note that the present record does **not** contain facts of arrest, warrant evasion, proclamation, recovery, or any other material to support a custodial-interrogation submission. So the prosecution does **not** overclaim that ground. Even so, the absence of any exceptional circumstance, coupled with the Supreme Court’s findings that the impugned relief was beyond jurisdiction, makes this a poor case for Section 438 protection.

### Judicial Explanation
This application is allowed because the verified record shows only a labour/service dispute about contract employees, not any case-specific material of arrest necessity, flight risk, or tampering by the applicants. The Supreme Court’s earlier ruling that the Labour Court acted beyond Sections 11-C/13-A confirms a jurisdictional error in the civil/service proceedings, but it does not convert this matter into one warranting denial of anticipatory bail. The Paragraph 24 exception to the absconding/proclamation line of cases is not engaged: there is no warrant, no proclamation, and no material of malicious or jurisdictionless coercive process. No individual overt acts are alleged against any particular applicant, so pre-arrest protection is appropriate on this record.

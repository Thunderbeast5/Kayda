### Judicial Explanation
The prosecution has established the core incident by the record: Ramesh Kumar is the accused, Suresh Gupta is the victim, and the incident summary specifically alleges that the accused stabbed the victim with a kitchen knife outside a restaurant. The evidence inventory further records two eyewitnesses as present at the scene and states that a blood-stained kitchen knife was recovered from the accused’s bag. Taken together, these facts support a finding that the accused used a dangerous weapon and caused hurt, which squarely brings the act within IPC 324. The setting and manner of the act also support the alternative and lesser ingredient of assault/criminal force under IPC 352.

The defense has correctly pointed out important gaps in the record. No medical report, injury certificate, doctor testimony, FIR, CCTV, or forensic report is on record, and there is also no formal seizure memo mentioned for the knife. These omissions prevent the Court from confidently elevating the matter to graver offences such as IPC 307 or IPC 326, because the record does not prove grievous injury, life-endangering harm, or the necessary degree of murderous intention beyond reasonable doubt.

However, the absence of medical and forensic documentation does not erase the direct allegation, the eyewitness presence, or the recovered blood-stained knife noted in the inventory. On the totality of the material available, the Court is satisfied that the prosecution has proved voluntary hurt by use of a dangerous weapon. The prosecution case is therefore accepted to the extent of IPC 324, while the higher aggravated charges fail for want of supporting medical and scientific proof.

Accordingly, the Court returns a finding of guilty under IPC 324, with IPC 352 also made out on the facts as an accompanying assault-based offence. The benefit of doubt is extended only as to any greater charge requiring stronger proof of grievous injury or specific murderous intent.

### Legal Theory
The prosecution’s case fails because the Labour Court was moved under a limited interpretive jurisdiction, yet it granted substantive regularisation and pay-scale relief without any standing order on record; the Supreme Court itself held that such relief could only be sought in a proper industrial reference, not under Section 11-C of the Uttar Pradesh Industrial Disputes Act and Section 13-A of the Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act.

### Submissions
1. The core defect is jurisdictional: the union approached the Labour Court under Section 11-C of the Uttar Pradesh Industrial Disputes Act and Section 13-A of the Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act [fact_3], but the relief sought was regularisation of 15 contract workers as substantive employees with consequential benefits [fact_4]. The Supreme Court has already held that the scope of Section 11-C / Section 13-A is narrower than a regular reference under Section 4-K of the Uttar Pradesh Industrial Disputes Act or Section 10 of the Industrial Disputes Act, and that the Labour Court could not grant such substantive relief in this proceeding [fact_14] [fact_15].
2. The record before the court does not contain any identified standing order of the Corporation that was actually interpreted or applied by the Labour Court; instead, the order relied on Rule 2 of the 1981 Service Regulations, which excludes contract employees [fact_16] [fact_9]. On that footing, the proceeding was converted from a standing-orders interpretation matter into an impermissible exercise of rewriting service rules, which the Labour Court had no power to do [fact_17].
3. The Corporation’s objection that the workers had not been selected through the regular recruitment process [fact_12] goes to the heart of the claim for regular status and regular pay scales [fact_6]. But the record before the court does not contain any selection record showing that the 15 workmen were appointed through the prescribed process [fact_12] [selection_process_record not_on_record in the evidence inventory]. Without proof of lawful recruitment, the claim to be treated as regular and substantive employees remains unsupported, and the claim to minimum wages in regular driver/conductor pay scales is equally unsustainable in this limited proceeding.

### Judicial Explanation
The record does not disclose any anticipatory-bail-type factual setting, and there is no individualized role allegation against a specific accused beyond the Corporation’s institutional challenge to the Labour Court’s jurisdiction. The paragraph 24 exception concerning jurisdictionless or malicious proclamations is inapplicable because there is no warrant/proclamation/absconding issue here; this is a concluded service dispute appeal. On the merits already settled by the Supreme Court, the Labour Court could not grant regularization or regular pay-scales under Section 11-C/Section 13-A, and the proper remedy was a regular industrial reference under Section 4-K or Section 10. Accordingly, the application is rejected.

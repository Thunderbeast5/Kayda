### Judicial Explanation
The application for anticipatory bail is rejected because the record discloses a prima facie grave violent assault involving a dangerous weapon. The case facts specifically allege that Ramesh Kumar stabbed Suresh Gupta with a kitchen knife outside a restaurant, and the victim was hospitalized. Two eyewitnesses are stated to be present, and the weapon is specifically said to have been recovered from the accused’s bag. On this material, the allegation is not a mere vague suspicion; it is a direct accusation of a serious stabbing incident.

Although the defense correctly points out that certain documents such as the FIR, medical report, seizure memo, and forensic report are not on record, those omissions do not eliminate the seriousness of the allegation at the anticipatory bail stage. The court is not conducting a final trial here; it must only assess whether custodial interrogation is reasonably necessary and whether the accusation is grave enough to deny pre-arrest protection. A knife attack causing hospitalization is, on its face, a serious offence.

The absence of medical and forensic papers may affect the ultimate charge under IPC 307 at trial, but it does not make the case fit for anticipatory bail on this record. The allegation of a stabbing with a kitchen knife, supported by eyewitness presence and weapon recovery, is sufficient to justify investigation in custody, especially to verify the assault sequence, the role of the accused, and the circumstances of the attack. The prosecution’s contention that custodial interrogation is needed is therefore accepted.

Accordingly, balancing the prima facie seriousness of the offence against the incomplete documentary record, the Court holds that this is not a fit case for pre-arrest bail. The application is DENIED.

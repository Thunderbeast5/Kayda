### [PROSECUTOR] (Round 1) · Citations: IPC 323, IPC 324, IPC 352, IPC 354, IPC 426, IPC 448, IPC 504, IPC 506, IPC 509, CrPC 438, CrPC 167 · Evidence: fact_1, fact_2, fact_3, fact_4, fact_5, fact_6, fact_7, fact_8, fact_9, fact_10, fact_11, fact_12, fact_13, fact_14, fact_15, fact_16, fact_17

### Legal Theory
May it please the Court, I strongly oppose this application for anticipatory bail. Section 438 CrPC is an extraordinary remedy, not a matter of routine. The record here discloses far more than a simple dispute: the FIR alleges that a group of named persons came to the complainant’s house, abused him and his mother in caste terms, assaulted him with an iron rod, pushed and beat the women in the house, threatened to burn the house with petrol bottles, damaged household property, and attacked the person who came to rescue them. These are serious allegations attracting IPC Sections 323, 324, 352, 426, 427, 448, 451, 452, 504, 506, and, on the complainant’s version, Section 3 of the SC/ST Act.

### Submissions
1. The Additional Sessions Judge has already recorded that there was specific caste abuse, that the complainant belongs to a Scheduled Caste community, and that independent witnesses witnessed the incident. In **Srikant Upadhyay v. State of Bihar**, the Supreme Court held that anticipatory bail is exceptional and should not be granted as a matter of course; it also emphasized that a person facing serious criminal process must submit to the authority of law. That principle applies with full force here.
2. Custodial interrogation is necessary for concrete reasons: the incident involves multiple named assailants, an iron rod, alleged petrol bottles, and damage to household property. The prosecution must confront the applicant with the specific role attributed to him, identify the roles of the co-accused, and secure the truth of the threats and assault. Further, the complainant, his mother, aunt, and a rescuer are all named in the narrative, so witness protection is a live concern if the applicant is granted pre-arrest protection.
3. In view of the gravity of the offence, statutory presumptions, and the necessity of custodial interrogation, the prosecution humbly prays that this Anticipatory Bail application be rejected.
- **Section 438 CrPC**: anticipatory bail; an exceptional pre-arrest remedy.
- **Section 167 CrPC**: investigative custody framework, relevant where effective interrogation is required during investigation.
- **IPC Section 323**: voluntarily causing hurt — requires proof of hurt and voluntary infliction.
- **IPC Section 324**: voluntarily causing hurt by dangerous weapons or means — here, the alleged **iron rod** is the relevant dangerous weapon.
- **IPC Section 352**: assault or criminal force otherwise than on grave provocation.
- **IPC Section 354**: assault/criminal force to woman with intent to outrage modesty — relevant to the alleged pulling of Mohini’s saree, if pressed on facts.
- **IPC Section 426 / 427**: mischief and mischief causing damage — relevant to the alleged damage to utensils and household items.
- **IPC Section 448 / 451 / 452**: house-trespass, trespass to commit offence, and trespass after preparation for hurt/assault/wrongful restraint — relevant because the FIR alleges entry into the house with iron rod and petrol bottles.
- **IPC Section 504**: intentional insult with intent to provoke breach of peace — relevant to caste abuse and threats.
- **IPC Section 506**: criminal intimidation — relevant to threats to burn the house and expel the family from the village.
- **IPC Section 509**: words/gestures intended to insult the modesty of a woman — potentially relevant to the allegations concerning Mohini.
- **Section 3 of the SC/ST Act**: the record shows allegations of caste-based abuse against a Scheduled Caste complainant in public view.
The FIR in this case is not limited to a simple quarrel. It alleges that on 25.11.2024, after the election results, a group including the present respondent came to the complainant’s house, abused him and his mother in caste terms, assaulted him with an iron rod, entered the house, manhandled the women, threatened to burn the house with petrol bottles, damaged household articles, and beat a friend who came to intervene. The Additional Sessions Judge found specific caste abuse, SC status of the complainant, and independent witnesses; these findings directly support the conclusion that the ingredients of the SC/ST allegation were prima facie made out.
4. The prosecution’s need for custody is not generic here. The case involves **multiple named assailants**, **an iron rod**, **alleged petrol bottles**, and **damage to household property**. Custodial interrogation is necessary to confront the applicant with the role attributed to him and to identify the part played by each co-accused in the group attack, the source and use of the weapon, and the circumstances of the threats to burn the house. Because the incident allegedly occurred in public view and independent witnesses are referenced, the applicant being on pre-arrest bail risks influencing witnesses and obstructing a free and fair investigation. The complainant and his family also allege threats to their residence and safety, which makes protection of witnesses a live concern.
5. The High Court’s view that the prosecution story is exaggerated cannot override the FIR’s detailed allegations at the anticipatory bail stage, especially where the record already reflects a caste-based insult, physical assault, and intimidation. On the facts as recorded, this is precisely the kind of case where Section 438 CrPC should not be used to neutralize investigation.

### Legal Theory
The prosecution version discloses a politically coloured group scuffle with allegations that are serious but materially contested; at the anticipatory bail stage, the record shows no medical report or other objective proof on file, and the prosecution’s case on specific overt acts, caste intent, and witness reliability remains vulnerable to challenge under the limited material presently on record.

### Submissions
1. The prosecution relies heavily on the FIR narrative and the Sessions Court’s prima facie view, but the High Court has already noted inconsistencies in the eye-witness account and treated the prosecution story as exaggerated and false [fact_23], [fact_25]. The alleged incident also occurred immediately after declaration of election results and had political overtones [fact_24], which materially weakens the inference of a deliberate caste-driven assault and supports the defense submission that the dispute may have arisen from a sudden political flashpoint rather than a premeditated criminal design [fact_4], [fact_16].
2. The record before the court does not contain a medical report, injury certificate, MLC, or hospital report; it only reflects that the complainant was given a medical treatment letter and sent to the Sub-District Hospital [fact_15]. The prosecution has failed to produce on record any forensic report or seizure material regarding the alleged iron rod, petrol bottles, or damaged articles, so the asserted seriousness of IPC 324 and IPC 427/BNS-linked allegations remains uncorroborated by objective evidence in the present record.
3. On the statutory ingredients, the allegations do not by themselves establish the requisite mens rea for every invoked provision. For IPC 504 and IPC 506, mere abusive words or threats in a heated election-related confrontation are not enough unless intentional provocation or a real criminal intimidation to cause alarm is shown with clarity [fact_4], [fact_13], [fact_24]. For IPC 324, the prosecution must still prove voluntary causing of hurt by a dangerous weapon, yet the record presently lacks medical evidence and independent corroboration of the precise role of the applicant [fact_7], [fact_15]. For the SC/ST Act allegation, although the Sessions Court noted caste abuse and SC status [fact_19], [fact_20], [fact_22], the High Court’s finding of inconsistencies and exaggeration [fact_23], [fact_25] creates a substantial doubt on whether the alleged caste abuse was the dominant animus or whether the episode was primarily a political altercation.

### Judicial Explanation
The record does not show any Section 82 proclamation, warrant non-compliance, or other absconding conduct, so the Paragraph 24 exception for jurisdictional abuse or malicious process does not apply. The FIR contains serious allegations, including caste abuse, assault with an iron rod, and threats to burn the house, but the present material is still largely uncorroborated by medical or forensic proof and does not demonstrate that custodial interrogation is indispensable. Individual roles are mixed and attributed to multiple co-accused, with the principal overt acts assigned to others rather than a clearly isolated role for the present applicant. In these circumstances, anticipatory bail can be granted with conditions, without expressing any view on guilt.

### Legal Theory
May it please the Court, I strongly oppose this application for anticipatory bail. The record demonstrates a clear and continuous pattern of evasion: summons were issued, then bailable warrants, then non-bailable warrants, and ultimately proclamation under Section 82 CrPC followed by proceedings under Section 83 CrPC. Even after all these coercive steps, the applicants did not appear before the Trial Court and did not surrender.

### Submissions
1. Under Section 482 BNSS [Section 438 CrPC], anticipatory bail is an extraordinary remedy, to be granted only in exceptional cases. The Supreme Court in this very matter has held that where an accused is absconding or concealing himself to avoid execution of a warrant, and proclamation under Section 82 CrPC has been issued, anticipatory bail is ordinarily not available. The Court further held that filing a bail application through counsel cannot be treated as appearance before the court when Section 82 and 83 proceedings are underway.
2. The allegations are grave. The FIR invokes Sections 341, 323, 354, 354B, 379, 504, 506 and 149 IPC and Sections 3 and 4 of the Prevention of Witch (Daain) Practices Act, 1999. The complaint alleges a coordinated assault on a woman, including humiliation, forcing dung into her mouth, and disrobing. This is not a case deserving pre-arrest indulgence.
3. The applicants’ conduct shows deliberate defiance of lawful process. They did not appear even after warrants and proclamation, and they cannot now ask this Court to neutralize the coercive process of law by granting anticipatory bail. Their custody and presence are necessary to preserve the authority of the Trial Court and to prevent further evasion.
4. In view of the gravity of the offence, statutory presumptions, and the necessity of custodial interrogation, the prosecution humbly prays that this Anticipatory Bail application be rejected.
- **Section 482 BNSS [Section 438 CrPC]** — anticipatory bail / pre-arrest bail; an exceptional discretionary remedy.
- **Section 187 BNSS [Section 167 CrPC]** — custodial remand framework, relevant to the prosecution’s claim that custody is needed to secure attendance and effective investigation.
- **Section 82 CrPC** — proclamation against a person who has absconded or is concealing himself so that warrant cannot be executed; requires prior issuance of warrant.
- **Section 83 CrPC** — attachment proceedings following proclamation.
- **Sections 341, 323, 354, 354B, 379, 504, 506 and 149 IPC** — as invoked in the FIR.
- **Sections 3 and 4 of the Prevention of Witch (Daain) Practices Act, 1999** — as invoked in the FIR.
- **Section 174 IPC** — non-attendance in obedience to an order from a public servant, including summons, notice, order or proclamation.
- **Section 174A IPC** — non-appearance in response to a proclamation under Section 82 CrPC.
The facts here are stark. The complaint alleges a group assault on a woman, including catching her by the hair, forcing dung into her mouth, and disrobing women. The case is not a trivial or technical dispute; it involves coordinated violence, humiliation, and theft, all of which justify strict judicial scrutiny.
5. More importantly for this bail application, the applicants did not submit to lawful process. Summons were issued, then bailable warrants, then non-bailable warrants, and ultimately proclamation under Section 82 CrPC and proceedings under Section 83 CrPC. Even after learning of these steps, the appellants did not appear, did not surrender, and did not take meaningful steps to regularize their position; instead, they kept pursuing bail while remaining absent.

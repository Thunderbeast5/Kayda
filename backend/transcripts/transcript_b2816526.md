- **[fact_9]** `[procedural_event]` Persons working on contract basis had earlier filed Writ Petition No. 41349 of 1999, Kanchi Lal and Others v. U.P.S.R.T.C., before the Allahabad High Court seeking the same benefits as regular employees, but that writ petition was dismissed. *(Source: para 5)*

### Submissions

The Supreme Court accepted this jurisdictional objection in [fact_13], [fact_14], [fact_16], and set aside the orders in [fact_17]. Without those foundational facts, the ingredients of IPC 174 or IPC 174A cannot even arise. ### Judicial Explanation
The record shows a labor/service dispute about whether 15 contract drivers and conductors could be treated as regular employees, and the Supreme Court held the Labour Court exceeded its jurisdiction in granting substantive regularization relief. However, for anticipatory bail purposes, the file contains no summons, warrant, proclamation, or specific allegation of non-appearance by any individual applicant, which are foundational facts for CrPC 82, IPC 174, or IPC 174A. Because the paragraph 24 exception for jurisdictionless or malicious process cannot be evaluated without any process record at all, the materials are insufficient to decide refusal or grant on a bail standard. The proper course is therefore NO_OPINION.

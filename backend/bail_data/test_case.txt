BAIL APPLICATION CASE

Facts:
The accused, a 45-year-old businessman with no prior criminal record, has been charged under Section 420 IPC (cheating) and Section 406 IPC (criminal breach of trust) in connection with a property transaction dispute. The complainant alleges that the accused entered into an agreement to purchase a commercial property worth Rs. 75 lakhs but failed to complete the transaction despite receiving an advance of Rs. 15 lakhs.

The accused contends that this is a purely civil dispute arising from a commercial transaction, and that the complainant has given it a criminal color to exert pressure. The accused states that he was unable to complete the transaction due to financial difficulties arising from the COVID-19 pandemic, and that he has offered to refund the advance amount with interest.

Current Status:
- Investigation is complete and chargesheet has been filed
- Accused has surrendered his passport voluntarily
- Accused has deep roots in the community, owning residential property and running a business in the city for 20 years
- No history of witness tampering or intimidation
- Accused has been cooperating with the investigation throughout
- The matter is currently at the stage of framing of charges
- Accused is seeking regular bail under Section 437 CrPC

Complainant's Position:
- The complainant argues that this is a serious economic offence involving misappropriation of Rs. 15 lakhs
- Claims that the accused has diverted the money for personal use
- States that the accused should remain in custody to prevent him from disposing of assets
- Argues that the accused has influential connections and may tamper with evidence

Defense Position:
- This is a commercial dispute arising from a failed business transaction
- The accused has a clean record and has always been law-abiding
- The accused has strong ties to the community and is not a flight risk
- Investigation is complete, so there is no risk of tampering with evidence
- The amounts involved are not of such magnitude as to deny bail
- The accused has the right to liberty under Article 21 of the Constitution
- Bail is the rule, jail is the exception
- The accused has already spent 45 days in custody

The defense seeks bail on the grounds that the offences are bailable, the accused is not a flight risk, investigation is complete, and continued detention is not justified given the nature of the case and the individual circumstances of the accused.

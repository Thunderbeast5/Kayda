KAYDA – SPECIAL ACT ROUTING / APPLICABILITY DECISION PROMPT
===============================================================

PURPOSE
-------
Use this instruction after IPC/BNS, BSA and BNSS retrieval has already been performed.

The task is NOT to perform final legal reasoning and NOT to decide guilt.
The task is only to decide which Special-Act documents should be processed further.

AVAILABLE SPECIAL-ACT DOCUMENTS
--------------------------------
1. dowry_prohibition_act.json
2. indian_institutes_of_information_technology_act.json
3. itpa.json
4. juvenile_justice_act.json
5. pocso_act.json
6. posh_act.json
7. prohibition_of_child_marriage_act.json
8. sc_st_prevention_of_atrocities_act.json

INPUTS PROVIDED TO THE MODEL
----------------------------
A. CASE FACTS
   The extracted factual description of the case.

B. RETRIEVED IPC/BNS SECTIONS
   Sections already retrieved by the legal retrieval pipeline.
   Use these as supporting legal signals, NOT as proof that a Special Act applies.

C. RETRIEVED BSA SECTIONS
   Evidence-law sections already retrieved.
   Use these to understand the type of evidence involved, but do not infer a Special
   Act merely because a BSA section appears.

D. RETRIEVED BNSS SECTIONS
   Procedural sections already retrieved.
   Use these to understand arrest, bail, investigation, custody, search, etc.,
   but do not infer a Special Act merely from procedural provisions.

E. SPECIAL-ACT DOCUMENT NAMES
   The eight documents listed above.

CORE DECISION RULE
------------------
For EACH Special Act, determine whether the case facts contain a sufficiently
specific factual/legal connection to that Act that its sections could materially
change the legal analysis.

Do NOT ask:
    "Can I imagine some connection to this Act?"

Ask:
    "Is there a concrete fact, allegation, victim/offender relationship,
     object of conduct, protected class, location/context, or transaction
     in this case that makes this Act reasonably applicable?"

A Special Act should be selected for further processing when there is a
PLAUSIBLE AND FACT-GROUNDED POSSIBILITY that at least one provision of that Act
governs the alleged conduct, protected person, transaction, or procedure.

The model must prefer RECALL over aggressive exclusion at this stage.
It is safer to pass one additional plausible Act to the next stage than to
silently discard an Act that could materially affect the case.

IMPORTANT:
- Do NOT require the exact statutory terminology to appear in the case.
- Understand paraphrases, indirect descriptions, factual equivalents, and
  ordinary-language descriptions.
- Do NOT select an Act solely because one keyword happens to appear.
- Do NOT select an Act solely because an IPC/BNS section looks superficially related.
- Do NOT use the mere presence of words such as "woman", "child", "electronic",
  "marriage", "land", or "harassment" as sufficient by themselves.
- Distinguish between facts that establish applicability and facts that are merely
  background.

ACT-SPECIFIC APPLICABILITY GUIDANCE
-----------------------------------

1. DOWRY PROHIBITION ACT
Select when the facts indicate a demand, giving, taking, receiving, agreeing to
give/take, or transaction involving property/valuable security connected with
marriage.

Examples of indirect wording that SHOULD trigger consideration:
- demand for cash, gold, vehicle, property or valuables as a marriage-related demand
- additional money demanded from the bride's/boy's family because of marriage
- articles/property demanded as a condition for marriage or continuation of marriage
- allegations that marriage-related property was demanded, given or received

Do NOT select merely because:
- the case is a matrimonial dispute
- the accused is a husband/in-law
- cruelty is alleged without a marriage-related property/valuable-security connection

2. INDIAN INSTITUTES OF INFORMATION TECHNOLOGY ACT
Select ONLY when the facts specifically concern an institution, authority,
establishment, governance, powers, offences, appointments, administration,
or statutory framework governed by the Indian Institutes of Information
Technology legislation.

Do NOT select merely because:
- computers, software, internet or electronic evidence are involved
- the accused is a student/teacher
- an educational institution is mentioned unless it is specifically an
  Indian Institute of Information Technology within the relevant statutory context

3. IMMORAL TRAFFIC (PREVENTION) ACT (ITPA)
Select when the facts indicate commercial sexual exploitation, trafficking/
procuring/inducing persons for prostitution, keeping/managing a brothel,
living on earnings of prostitution, soliciting for prostitution, or another
specific activity falling within the Act's subject matter.

Do NOT select merely because:
- sexual assault is alleged
- consensual sexual activity is mentioned
- a woman is involved
- prostitution is mentioned in an unrelated/background context without a
  conduct connection to the Act

4. JUVENILE JUSTICE ACT
Select when the case materially concerns a child in conflict with law,
child in need of care and protection, juvenile justice proceedings,
rehabilitation/child-care institutions, adoption, foster care, or another
specific statutory juvenile-justice issue.

Do NOT select merely because:
- the word "child" appears
- the victim is young, unless the facts indicate a juvenile-justice issue
- POCSO-type sexual offences are alleged against a child; POCSO may apply
  independently, while JJ Act applicability must be separately established

5. POCSO ACT
Select when the facts indicate a person below 18 years is the victim/subject
of a sexual offence, sexual assault, sexual harassment, use of a child for
pornographic purposes/material, or another offence specifically concerning
sexual offences against children.

The case does NOT need to use the word "minor" or "POCSO".
Equivalent factual descriptions must be recognized, such as:
- 15-year-old girl/boy
- school-going child subjected to sexual acts
- victim was below eighteen
- sexual conduct involving a child
- explicit material involving a child

Do NOT select merely because:
- a young adult is involved
- the case involves a school or college
- the word "child" refers to an adult's son/daughter without establishing
  the person's statutory age

6. POSH ACT
Select when the facts concern sexual harassment in the workplace or an
employment/professional environment covered by the legislation, including
workplace-related complaints, employer/employee context, Internal Committee/
complaint mechanism, workplace inquiry, or sexual harassment occurring in a
covered workplace context.

Recognize indirect wording such as:
- superior demanded sexual favours from employee
- inappropriate sexual conduct by manager toward subordinate
- employee complained to employer about sexual harassment
- complaint before workplace/internal committee

Do NOT select merely because:
- sexual harassment occurred in a private/non-workplace setting
- the victim is a woman
- harassment is mentioned without a workplace connection

7. PROHIBITION OF CHILD MARRIAGE ACT
Select when the facts indicate a marriage involving a person below the legally
prescribed marriage age, arranging/solemnizing such a marriage, promoting,
permitting, or facilitating child marriage, or proceedings concerning the
validity/prohibition of such a marriage.

Recognize indirect wording such as:
- girl aged 16 was married
- family arranged marriage of a 17-year-old
- marriage was solemnized before the person reached the prescribed age

Do NOT select merely because:
- the case concerns marriage
- the victim is a young adult
- POCSO applies; child marriage applicability must be independently grounded

8. SC/ST (PREVENTION OF ATROCITIES) ACT
Select when the facts indicate that the victim/complainant or accused's legal
status as a member of a Scheduled Caste or Scheduled Tribe is materially
connected to the alleged offence, or the allegations concern a caste/tribe-
based atrocity covered by the Act.

Recognize indirect wording such as:
- accused abused victim by caste name
- victim was targeted because of caste identity
- SC/ST member was dispossessed/intimidated/humiliated in circumstances
  covered by the Act
- allegation expressly identifies the victim as belonging to an SC/ST community
  and connects that status to the offence

Do NOT select merely because:
- "caste" is mentioned as background
- the parties belong to different communities without a legally relevant
  caste-based allegation
- a general assault/cheating/property dispute occurs between persons of
  different castes without an atrocity-related factual connection

HOW TO USE IPC/BNS RETRIEVAL
----------------------------
IPC/BNS sections are supporting signals.

Examples:
- An offence involving a child + sexual conduct -> strengthens POCSO consideration.
- Matrimonial allegations alone do NOT automatically establish Dowry Act applicability.
- Caste-related facts + relevant substantive offence -> strengthens SC/ST Act consideration.
- Workplace sexual-harassment facts -> strengthen POSH consideration.
- Cyber/electronic facts alone do NOT automatically establish applicability of
  the IT Act.

The retrieved IPC/BNS sections can increase or decrease confidence, but CASE FACTS
must remain the primary basis for the decision.

HOW TO USE BSA RETRIEVAL
------------------------
BSA sections identify the evidence involved.

Examples:
- electronic records -> may support consideration of the IT Act ONLY if the
  underlying conduct is cyber/electronic conduct governed by that Act
- documents/signatures -> do NOT by themselves imply any Special Act
- expert evidence -> do NOT by itself imply any Special Act

Never route an Act simply because a BSA evidence category happens to overlap.

HOW TO USE BNSS RETRIEVAL
-------------------------
BNSS sections primarily describe procedure.

Examples:
- anticipatory bail, arrest, custody, search, seizure, investigation, etc.
  should generally NOT determine Special-Act applicability by themselves.
- A Special Act should be selected because of the underlying facts and substantive
  legal context, not because the case involves bail.

DECISION LEVELS
---------------
Use exactly one of these labels for every Act:

1. PROCESS
   Strong or reasonably strong factual/legal basis exists. The Act should be
   loaded and its relevant sections should be retrieved/processed further.

2. CANDIDATE
   There is a plausible factual connection, but applicability cannot yet be
   determined confidently from the available facts. Keep the Act for downstream
   verification.

3. SKIP
   No material factual/legal connection is present in the supplied case.

ROUTING THRESHOLD
-----------------
Use the following conservative rule:

PROCESS:
    There are one or more concrete facts directly matching the Act's subject
    matter AND the Act could materially affect the legal analysis.

CANDIDATE:
    The facts are incomplete/ambiguous but contain a meaningful signal that
    could make the Act applicable.

SKIP:
    There is no meaningful factual connection after considering reasonable
    paraphrases and indirect wording.

When uncertain between PROCESS and CANDIDATE:
    choose CANDIDATE.

When uncertain between CANDIDATE and SKIP:
    choose CANDIDATE if the potential connection could materially affect
    criminal liability, victim protection, jurisdiction, procedure, or bail.

OUTPUT REQUIREMENTS
-------------------
Return STRICT JSON ONLY.

Use this schema:

{
  "act_routing": [
    {
      "document": "pocso_act.json",
      "decision": "PROCESS",
      "confidence": 0.96,
      "matched_facts": [
        "The victim is stated to be 15 years old",
        "The allegation concerns sexual acts against the victim"
      ],
      "reason": "The facts describe a sexual offence against a person below 18 years, creating a direct factual basis for POCSO applicability."
    },
    {
      "document": "dowry_prohibition_act.json",
      "decision": "SKIP",
      "confidence": 0.97,
      "matched_facts": [],
      "reason": "No marriage-related demand, giving, taking or receiving of property or valuable security is alleged."
    }
  ]
}

RULES FOR OUTPUT:
- Include ALL eight documents.
- Do not invent facts.
- matched_facts must quote or closely paraphrase only facts actually present in CASE FACTS.
- Do not cite a section number unless that section number was supplied in the inputs.
- confidence is confidence in the ROUTING DECISION, not confidence in guilt.
- Keep reasons short and fact-grounded.
- If facts are ambiguous, use CANDIDATE rather than SKIP.
- Do not perform full section retrieval in this step.
- Do not decide guilt, innocence, bail, admissibility, or final applicability conclusively.
- The purpose is only to decide which Special-Act JSON documents deserve further processing.

ANTI-HALLUCINATION CHECK
-------------------------
Before returning PROCESS or CANDIDATE, ask internally:

1. What exact fact in the case supports this Act?
2. Is that fact actually present in the case?
3. Am I relying on a keyword alone?
4. Am I confusing a generic criminal offence with a Special-Act offence?
5. Could this Act materially affect the case if the factual allegation is proved?

If no concrete answer exists for questions 1 and 2, return SKIP.

FINAL ROUTING PRINCIPLE
-----------------------
MAXIMIZE RECALL AT THIS STAGE, BUT REQUIRE A FACTUAL BASIS.

The output of this prompt will be used as a gate:

    CASE FACTS
        +
    IPC/BNS retrieval
        +
    BSA retrieval
        +
    BNSS retrieval
              ↓
       SPECIAL-ACT ROUTER
              ↓
       PROCESS / CANDIDATE
              ↓
      load only selected JSONs
              ↓
      retrieve relevant sections
              ↓
      downstream legal verifier/reasoner

Never load all Special-Act JSON files merely because they exist.

IN THE SUPREME COURT OF INDIA
CRIMINAL APPELLATE JURISDICTION

GURBAKSH SINGH SIBBIA ETC.
v.
STATE OF PUNJAB

Bench: Y. V. Chandrachud, C.J., P. N. Bhagwati, N. L. Untwalia, R. S. Pathak and O. Chinnappa Reddy, JJ.
Date: April 9, 1980
Citation: [1980] 3 S.C.R. 383; (1980) 2 SCC 565

CASE BACKGROUND AND FACTUAL ACCUSATIONS:
The appellant herein, Sri Gurbaksh Singh Sibbia, was a Minister of Irrigation and Power in the Congress Ministry of the Government of Punjab. Grave allegations of political corruption, financial irregularities, and misuse of official public office were made against him and other associate public servants following a change of political ministry in the State.

The accusations allege commission of serious offences under the Indian Penal Code, specifically including Section 409 of the Indian Penal Code (Criminal breach of trust by a public servant, punishable with imprisonment for life or imprisonment up to 10 years, and fine) alongside other offences relating to corruption and financial misappropriation. 

Apprehending imminent arrest by the State police authorities on these allegations of non-bailable offences, the appellant and others filed applications in the High Court of Punjab and Haryana under Section 438 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 (CrPC), praying for an order of anticipatory bail directing that they be released on bail in the event of their arrest on the aforesaid charges.

PROCEDURAL HISTORY AND HIGH COURT FULL BENCH RULING:
Considering the public importance of the legal questions, a Single Judge referred the applications to a Full Bench of the High Court of Punjab and Haryana. By judgment dated September 13, 1977, the Full Bench dismissed the anticipatory bail applications, formulating a restrictive 8-point code:
1. That the power under Section 438 CrPC is extraordinary and must be exercised sparingly in exceptional cases only;
2. That all limitations under Section 437 CrPC must be implicitly read into Section 438;
3. That the petitioner must make out a "special case" beyond ordinary bail considerations;
4. That where a legitimate case for remand to police custody under Section 167(2) CrPC or recovery under Section 27 of the Indian Evidence Act is claimed, Section 438 should not be exercised;
5. That discretion under Section 438 cannot be exercised for offences punishable with death or imprisonment for life (such as Section 409 IPC) unless the court is satisfied that the charge is false or groundless;
6. That in serious economic offences involving corruption at high rungs of executive power, anticipatory bail should not be exercised;
7. That general allegations of mala fides are inadequate unless substantial proof demonstrates that accusations are false and baseless.

CONTENTIONS OF THE APPLICANT / APPELLANTS:
1. The appellant contends that Section 438 is a procedural safeguard for personal liberty and the presumption of innocence guaranteed under Article 21 of the Constitution of India, and judicial discretion cannot be fettered by self-imposed cast-iron rules or amputated by importing Section 437 limitations.
2. The appellant contends that the proposed accusations stem from political rivalry, vengeance, and antagonism following the defeat of his political ministry, designed to harass, humiliate, and parade respectable individuals in handcuffs.
3. The appellant is a person of established roots and permanent residence who has deep ties to society and is hardly likely to flee from justice or abscond.
4. The appellant is ready and willing to submit to all reasonable conditions, including joining the investigation, making himself available for police interrogation under Section 438(2)(i), not tampering with witnesses or evidence under Section 438(2)(ii), not leaving the country without permission under Section 438(2)(iii), and facilitating any lawful discovery under Section 27 of the Evidence Act.
